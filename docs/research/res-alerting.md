## 1. What established safety systems do: fan-out, ack windows, escalation

**Medical alert pendants (PERS).** The canonical pattern: button press → two-way voice to a staffed monitoring centre → if the user is reachable, they steer response; **if there is no response, the centre escalates anyway** — "assess the emergency situation and contact designated family members or emergency services" (AARP's buyer guide on how these systems work). Note the architecture: the *device never talks only to a phone*; it talks to a 24/7 staffed hub, and phones are a fan-out target, not the decision point. False alarms are explicitly designed for ("it's not uncommon for people to push the help button accidentally"; unlimited-false-alarm plans are a selling feature). — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/caregiving/home-care/medic-alert-systems-options/); [NCOA medical alert reviews, 2026](https://www.ncoa.org/product-resources/medical-alert-systems/best-medal-alert-systems/) ([link](https://www.ncoa.org/product-resources/medical-alert-systems/best-medical-alert-systems/))

**Fire/intruder alarm monitoring (standards-level, most rigorous).** EN 50136-1:2012 defines graded alarm-transmission paths: single-path classes SP1–SP4 and dual-path DP1–DP4, with **mandatory maximum fault-notification times** (e.g., SP3 ≈ 30 min, SP4 ≈ 3 min to report a broken path; dual paths are supervised independently — the system must *know* within seconds-to-minutes if the primary route dies and fail over to the backup). — [evalink EN 50136 summary](https://documentation.evalink.io/talos/admin/work-with-integrations/alarm-transmitter-integrations/alarm-transmitter-connection-monitoring/en-50136-standard/), [Kiwa NCP certification note](https://www.kiwa.com/globalassets/netherlands/kiwa-ncp/pdf/k21030-04-alarm-transmission-systems.pdf), [standard page](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/f655e4d2-aa75-419f-8e2d-f971b0790359/en-50136-1-2012). UL 985 governs household fire/medical-alert units in the US. Lesson: **transmission-path supervision + automatic failover is codified engineering practice, not optional polish.**

**Panic-button apps with staffed back-end (Noonlight).** A field journalist test (CBS Philadelphia, 2019) simulated an incapacitated user: press, ignore the 10-sec duress window, ignore all follow-up calls/texts. Resulting cascade: automated contact attempt at ~40 s, reached dispatch at ~2 min, police deployed at ~4 min — and the app's location was ~¼ mile off, forcing responders to pull GPS from a separate site. Officials' verdict: "911 is the only way to go." — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/testing-popular-safety-app-noonlights-response-time-compared-to-911/). Lesson: even a professionally run cascade takes **minutes**, and app-mediated location can be stale/wrong.

**Lone-worker devices.** Commercial platforms document the "no ack = escalate" ladder explicitly: alarm → device/user check-in window → if unacknowledged, escalate to a supervisor contact list → then to a monitoring centre that can call the worker and/or dispatch. Examples with public escalation-config docs: [Mitti escalation help](https://help.mitti.com/004648), [LWS lone-worker escalation protocols](https://lwstech.com/blog/lone-worker-alarms/), [Neovigie VigieControl alert centre](https://www.neovigie.com/en-us/alert-center). (I could not find primary technical documentation for "NKBA/RedButton" or "FallCheck" specifically — searches returned only noise; treat them as unverifiable vendor examples, and use Noonlight/lone-worker docs above instead as your pattern sources.)

**GPS fleet SOS.** On the cheap-tracker class you plan to use (TKSTAR/JT518-type, JT808 protocol), the SOS button's native behavior is: place a **voice call to the preset centre number** *and* send SMS/online alarm packets to configured numbers/platform — i.e., the device itself already fans out call+SMS+data in parallel; your platform should add escalation on top of the alarm event (Traccar receives it as an SOS/alarm position). Manuals: [TKSTAR manuals](https://www.tk-star.com/?m=common&a=down&id=26), [Traccar forums on SMS vs GPRS mode](https://www.traccar.org/forums/topic/how-to-get-gprs-data-instead-of-via-sms/).

**Public child-alert systems (AMBER-type).** Broadcast + mobile alerting operated by police/broadcasters/transport; ICMEC/Global Missing Kids catalog them by country. — [ICMEC child alerts](https://www.icmec.org/global-missing-childrens-center/child-alerts/), [Global Missing Kids](https://globalmissingkids.org/rapid-emergency-child-alert-systems/), [Wikipedia AMBER alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_alert).

**Extracted patterns (the industry-common skeleton):**
1. Alert never depends on one channel — voice call + SMS + push fire **in parallel**, not sequentially-optional.
2. Every alarm has an **acknowledgment window**; unacknowledged ≠ ignored, it *triggers escalation*, not cancellation.
3. Escalation cascades through a contact list (parent 1 → parent 2 → backup → staffed centre → public authority).
4. The transmission path itself is **supervised** (heartbeat); a dead path is an alarm in itself (EN 50136).
5. Human-in-the-loop monitoring centre is the last-resort fallback precisely because phones fail (do not disturb, dead battery, killed app).

## 2. Android push: measured failure/delay modes and documented workarounds

**Platform-documented behavior (authoritative):**
- **Doze** "suspends network access, ignores wake locks, defers standard AlarmManager alarms (including `setExact()`) to the next maintenance window"; maintenance windows become **less frequent over time** during inactivity; idle apps' network access can be allowed only **"about once a day"**. `setAndAllowWhileIdle()` may fire at most **once per 9 minutes per app**; only `setAlarmClock()` reliably exits Doze. Crucially, Google's own policy table lists **"Safety Apps" as a legitimate core-function case for the battery-optimization exemption** (`ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`). — [Optimize for Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)
- **FCM priority**: "normal" messages are explicitly *delayed until the device leaves Doze*; "high" is meant for urgent alerts only and briefly wakes the device — but delivery still traverses Google Play Services, which can be killed by OEMs. — [Firebase FCM message priority docs](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android-message-priority), [Firebase blog on FCM delivery (2024/2025)](https://firebase.blog/posts/2024/07/understand-fcm-delivery-rates/) ([2025 post](https://firebase.blog/posts/2025/04/fcm-on-android/)), [Pushwoosh on Doze non-delivery](https://help.pushwoosh.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018576251-Why-don-t-Android-devices-receive-push-notifications-in-Doze-power-saving-mode)
- **OEM process killers**: dontkillmyapp.com (maintained by Urbandroid, with a benchmark Play Store app) catalogs 17+ OEMs — Huawei, Xiaomi, OnePlus, Samsung, Oppo, Vivo, Realme, Motorola… — that "prefer battery life over proper functionality of your apps," breaking foreground services and notifications long after stock-Android mitigations are applied. Real reports include Huawei killing even correctly-implemented foreground services. — [dontkillmyapp.com](https://dontkillmyapp.com/), [StackOverflow Huawei case](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/56883074/huawei-device-killing-my-foreground-service-even-with-dontkillmyapp-coms-solut), [biolovision per-brand survival guide](https://help.biolovision.net/Keep_Background_Services_Alive_on_Android_(by_Brand))
- **Non-GMS devices** (post-ban Huawei/HarmonyOS phones sold in Algeria): FCM **cannot be delivered at all** (no Play Services); you must use HMS Push or your own socket. — [Pushy non-GMS](https://pushy.me/non-gms), [Huawei developer forum](https://forums.developer.huawei.com/forumPortal/en/topic/0201284904227550043)

**SMS / voice vs push.** SMS rides the circuit-switched signalling channel: it needs no data plan, no app alive, and is store-and-forward in the SMSC (survives temporary no-coverage); it typically arrives in 1–5 s when in coverage. Voice calls are even harder to suppress and get a human's attention (open-rate evidence in emergency-notification marketing: [DialMyCalls SMS vs voice](https://www.dialmycalls.com/blog/sms-vs-voice-call-for-emergency-alerts)). The weak links of SMS: spam-filtering of A2P traffic and sender-ID costs in some countries ([ACM study on SMS reliability in notification scenarios, 2025/2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3801359.3801392)). For *medical/child* alerting, the literature treats SMS+call as the floor and push as the accelerator. Push alone is unsuitable: it requires connectivity + a living app process + OEM cooperation. ([SMS Eagle on going offline](https://www.smseagle.eu/2025/01/15/reliable-notifications-in-a-digital-world-going-offline/))

**Documented workarounds (composite from the above sources):**
- Battery-optimization exemption (allowed for safety apps) + per-OEM autostart/whitelist screens ([dontkillmyapp brand pages](https://dontkillmyapp.com/))
- Persistent `foregroundService` with `location` type + high-priority FCM payloads ([Android docs above](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby))
- `setAlarmClock()` for watchdogs; WebSocket/MQTT own-channel with reconnect, so you don't depend solely on FCM
- In-app "heartbeat" telemetry: server-side per-device delivery receipts + last-seen; a parent phone silent >N min = degraded-alert state that forces SMS+call fan-out *bypassing push entirely*
- Dual-SIM parents; don't use Huawei-without-GMS as a primary alert phone (or use it only for SMS/call).

## 3. When the child's tracker has no internet / no data on the SIM

Usable fallbacks, ordered for Algeria:

1. **SMS mode is native on your device class.** TKSTAR/JT-style trackers work in "GPRS (online)" or **"SMS (offline command)" mode**: configuration, position requests, and alarms are SMS to/from the tracker's number ([WhiteLabelTracking GPRS-vs-SMS comparison](https://www.whitelabeltracking.com/gprs-commands-vs-sms-commands-how-they-work-and-which-to-choose-for-your-tracking-device/), [Traccar forum](https://www.traccar.org/forums/topic/how-to-get-gprs-data-instead-of-via-sms/)). SMS requires no data plan and no APN — only CS signal. This is the single most important fact for "SIM has no data/balance": **keep a 2G-capable tracker and treat SMS as a peer channel, not a degraded one.**
2. **Store-and-forward.** Trackers buffer positions when offline and replay them on reconnection ("live and buffered data" — [gpsgate/Traccar docs](https://support.gpsgate.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005346814-Live-and-buffered-data), [Teltonika forum on recovery after GSM/GPRS loss](https://community.teltonika.lt/t/data-recovery-after-gsm-gprs-signal-loss/6699)). Buffering covers *trajectory* but only helps delivery when signal returns — design must distinguish "cache is intact, link is down" from "child is safe."
3. **Voice call from the tracker.** JT518-class SOS places an actual call to the centre number even in poor-data conditions; a circuit-switched call succeeds where GPRS attach fails. Cheap, already in the device.
4. **USSD.** Session-based, works over CS signalling with **no data subscription and no GPRS** — widely used for mobile money precisely for that reason ([Pelion USSD-in-IoT guide](https://pelion.com/knowledge-base/ussd), [Telerivet](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/what-is-ussd), [Wireless Logic](https://wirelesslogic.com/iot-glossary/what-is-ussd/)). Caveats: needs a commercial USSD gateway contract with each Algerian operator, initiated-from-network (the tracker cannot reliably *pull* a USSD session on its own for machine-type use), 182-char sessions. Verdict: possible, operator-dependent, expensive integration; SMS is usually "good enough" where USSD shines.
5. **Constrained-IP protocols (CoAP / MQTT-SN).** Reduce *data-plane* latency/robustness issues on 2.5G — but if there is no PDP context, no protocol helps. They solve "weak/slow GPRS", not "no data". (Protocol references: [CoAP](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7252), [MQTT-SN](https://mqtt.org/mqtt-sq/))
6. **Emergency call without SIM/data.** GSM/3GPP lets a phone dial the emergency number with no SIM/no registration by attaching to *any* visible network as "emergency-only." Real limits (no callback number for the PSAP, network-dependent, VoLTE/IMS migration complicates it): [GSMA technical blog on emergency calls](https://www.gsma.com/get-involved/working-groups/content-type/technical-blog/quarterly-blog-emergency-calls/), [EENA on emergency over LTE/IMS](https://eena.org/document/emergency-communications-over-lte-and-ims-unresolved-issues-and-risks/), [heise/e112 location](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Emergency-Call-Day-112-No-rescue-without-location-data-11172941.html). Cheap trackers generally *won't* do emergency-only dialing (they need the SIM's own registration) — don't count on it.
7. **Multi-IMSI / multi-network IoT SIMs.** SIM stores several operator profiles and rovers to a second network when its home network is down — the standard fix for single-operator coverage gaps ([Onomondo technical dive](https://onomondo.com/blog/what-is-multi-imsi-sim-for-iot/), [ixt.io](https://ixt.io/blog/what-is-multi-imsi-failover-in-iot-connectivity), [Hologram multi-IMSI vs multi-core](https://www.hologram.io/blog/connectivity-face-off-multi-imsi-vs-multi-core-sims/)). In Algeria: no public LoRaWAN network exists (LoRaWAN operators map ~100 countries, none Algerian — [LoRa Alliance](https://resources.lora-alliance.org/lora-alliance-news/lora-alliance-passes-100-lorawan-network-operator-milestone-with-coverage-in-100-countries)), so LoRa is out unless you build your own gateways (expensive, city-limited). Satellite trackers (Iridium/ACN, e.g., inReach) are globally covered but **imports of satellite comms equipment are legally restricted in many countries including Algeria** — Garmin's own travel FAQ and traveller reports warn about confiscation/fines ([Garmin FAQ](https://support.garmin.com/en-US/?faq=BPr8T6bFcN3uxv59Cfqmq9), [FoxNomad warning](https://foxnomad.com/2025/01/23/beware-of-traveling-to-certain-countries-with-a-garmin-inreach/)); treat satellite as a non-voluntary consumer option there.
8. **Algerian operator reality.** 2G/3G/4G all live; 5G commercial launch 2025–26 with regulator-mandated coverage in all 58 provinces; regulators have **fined Mobilis, Djezzy and Ooredoo for weak 4G coverage**, and rural south is sparse ([Ookla on North-Africa 5G, 2026](https://www.ookla.com/articles/5g-north-africa-2026), [Opensignal Algeria report](https://insights.opensignal.com/reports/2023/03/algeria/mobile-network-experience), [Developing Telecoms on 4G fines](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-business/telecom-regulation/10080-algeria-issues-operator-fines-for-weak-4g-coverage.html), [Telecom in Algeria (2G GSM still in service)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Algeria)). → Pick a **2G (GSM+SMS) tracker** so it works where only 2G is usable, and put a *different* operator's SIM in the tracker than the parents' primary phones if you can.

## 4. Device destroyed / removed / jammed

**Off-body & tamper detection that actually ships:**
- **Correctional ankle monitors** (BI Incorporated et al.): tamper alarms from strap removal (mechanical/electrical loop in the band), plus RF "check-in" supervision; false-tamper handling is a known product problem ([BI GPS docs](https://bi.com/gps/), [tamper-tech overview](https://www.ankle-monitor.com/blog/2026/04/19/ankle-monitor-false-alarm-tamper-detection-credibility-2026/)). For your design the transferable primitives are: **strap-continuity switch** (cheap) + **wrist-removal telemetry**.
- **Consumer wearables**: Apple Watch uses optical + capacitive skin sensing for wrist detection (its failure modes — arm hair, lotion — are documented: [Apple 2015 sensor change after arm-hair misreads](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ani/apple-removes-sensors-from-smartwatch-after-arm-hair-disrupts-readings-115021801007_1.html)). Optical removal detection on a dark-skin/high-hair population has real false-positive risk → combine **PPG presence + strap switch + motion signature**, and never let "sensor off-wrist" alone cancel an abduction alarm (fail open).
- **BLE disconnect watchdog** (any tag paired to a phone/child's secondary device): timeout → alarm. Cheap, already used in Tile/AirTag-style anti-separation products.
- **"Device not worn" alarms** exist in lone-worker/health wearables (no HR signal + no motion = check-in escalation).

**Jamming — what's documented:** cheap ($25–50) ignition-key GPS jammers flood GPS L1 (1.575 GHz) creating 10–50 m denial bubbles; "upgraded models" also block cellular (2G/4G) and Wi-Fi bands; fleet platforms respond with **loss-of-GPS alerts, last-known-position logic, and independent BLE-mesh reporting** ([Airpinpoint fleet-jammer analysis](https://airpinpoint.com/blog/gps-tracker-jammer-fleet), [Geotab on jammers](https://www.geotab.com/blog/combating-gps-jammers/), [Verizon Connect](https://www.verizonconnect.com/resources/article/gps-jammers/)). Practical device-side defenses at low cost:
- Treat **GPS-loss-under-motion** and **cell-signal-loss + no-registration** as *alarms themselves* (silent-abduction detectors), not just telemetry gaps.
- Cache last known position + recent trajectory in flash and transmit on reconnect; include in every alarm the age of the position.
- IMU dead-reckoning: a tracker that keeps *moving with vibration patterns* while its GPS freezes is more suspicious than a static one — vibration/motion sensor events are standard on TKSTAR-class devices (SOS + vibration alarm inputs).
- Accept honestly: a $50 broadband jammer defeats a $20 tracker completely. Your defense is **human escalation on absence-of-evidence** (device silent > N minutes while alarm state active → treat as worst case: call parents *and* the police report pathway, not "retry later"). Jammer possession/use is itself a serious crime in most jurisdictions — flagging the "signal died abruptly" event creates evidence.

## 5. Latency budget — how fast must this be?

Published time-criticality evidence:
- **76% of abducted-then-murdered children were killed within 3 hours** of abduction; 88.5% within 24 h (2006 study, cited by Washington State AG). — [WA ATG child-abduction murder research](https://www.atg.wa.gov/child-abduction-murder-research) — corroborated by [NCMEC-derived analyses in the DOJ "Case Management for Missing Children Homicide Investigation" guide](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/218936NCJRS.pdf) and the FBI's Rapid Deployment program which exists *because* the first hours dominate outcomes ([FBI LEB spotlight, 2011 — blocked to fetch, cite from search list](https://leb.fbi.gov/spotlights/crimes-against-children-spotlight-child-abduction-rapid-deployment-card-team)).
- Canadian replicated study of abducted-then-murdered children reaches the same "first hours are decisive" conclusion. — [Missing Kids Canada / ProtectChildren study, 2016](https://missingkids.ca/en/media-centre/news-releases/2016/mcd_amc)
- The only European child-alert research (Portmouth, 4 countries, 19 of 23 alerts in 2015): **about half of alerted children were found dead**; the authors stress existing processes "wasn't always fast enough" — [Shalev-Greene & Hedges 2016 via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016-03-160323082716.htm) ([original PDF now redirects](https://www.amberalert.eu/))
- Counter-example of what a non-optimized cascade costs: **~4 minutes to dispatch** in the Noonlight field test, *after a human pressed a button* — [CBS test above](https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/testing-popular-safety-app-noonlights-response-time-compared-to-911/).

**Engineering conclusion.** The adversary is not "minutes matter," it is "the first **hour** decides who lives, and the response chain (parents → operators → police → roadblocks) eats tens of minutes even when perfect." So:
- **Child→server (tracker alarm event): budget ≤ 30 s** (SMS mode 2–5 s delivery; GPRS attach on 2G can take 5–15 s; JT808 keepalive intervals must be ≤ 1–2 min so a lost device is detected fast).
- **Server→first parent informed: budget ≤ 30 s** (parallel fan-out, high-priority push + SMS + call placed immediately; *not* push-then-wait-then-SMS).
- **All guardians + backup contacts reached or declared degraded: ≤ 2–3 min**; auto-generated "call the police now + this location + this description" script delivered in the first message.
- Ack windows for *escalation logic* should be short (30–60 s), because "acknowledged" by a sleeping parent is worthless vs "parent woke up and is dialing 17."
- Design choices that move the end-to-end number most, in order: (1) removing any *serial* dependency on push delivery, (2) making SMS/call fan-out parallel and automatic, (3) keeping the tracker on 2G-capable SMS-capable hardware, (4) heartbeat supervision so link-death triggers worst-case escalation instead of silence.

**What a parent can realistically request from authorities** (no Algerian public procedure documentation found — nearest public templates: Morocco/France practice and the EU/US missing-child investigator guides: [DOJ Investigator's Guide to Missing Child Cases](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/108768NCJRS.pdf), [College of Policing missing-persons initial-contact doctrine](https://www.college.police.uk/app/missing-persons/initial-contact) which your UX can borrow: no waiting period, immediate risk assessment). Concretely in Algeria: file a *plainte/main courante* at the nearest commissariat or gendarmerie immediately (abduction of a minor = flagrant offence, prosecutor acts); ask the prosecutor to order (a) **CCTV preservation** from shops/banks on the route (operators/cities keep footage only days — speed matters, build this into your app's checklist), (b) **operator cell-site (historique de bornes) data** for the child's tracker SIM and any suspected phone — this is only obtainable *by judicial requisition to the ARPT/operators*, never by the parents directly; (c) roadblock/border-point alerts. Your product should generate a one-tap **incident sheet** (timestamps, last positions w/ cell IDs, photos, description) formatted to hand to police — that materially speeds requisitions. (Algeria telecom law requires operators to retain traffic data; enforcement varies — flag as assumption to verify locally.)

## 7. Testing methodology for real-world reliability

- **Standards-derived acceptance:** define your alarm-transport classes like EN 50136 (SP/DP) and set max fault-notification times per class; measure them ([EN 50136 docs above](https://documentation.evalink.io/talos/admin/work-with-integrations/alarm-transmitter-integrations/alarm-transmitter-connection-monitoring/en-50136-standard/), [Kiwa](https://www.kiwa.com/globalassets/netherlands/kiwa-ncp/pdf/k21030-04-alarm-transmission-systems.pdf)).
- **Device × OEM matrix field trials:** run on dontkillmyapp-affected brands explicitly (Huawei non-GMS, Xiaomi, Samsung, Oppo) with their autostart settings both on/off — the site + its benchmark app is literally the published protocol for this class of failure ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/), [benchmark app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.urbandroid.dontkillmyapp)).
- **Scenario replay / red-team the alert:** the CBS Noonlight protocol is the template: incapacitated victim, ignored ack windows, wrong-location injection, response-time measured end to end ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/philadelphia/news/testing-popular-safety-app-noonlights-response-time-compared-to-911/)).
- **Coverage/latency mapping in Algeria:** OpenSignal/nPerf per-operator coverage maps to pick the tracker SIM; drive-test routes in your cities recording GPRS attach time, SMS delivery time, dead zones ([Opensignal Algeria](https://insights.opensignal.com/reports/2023/03/algeria/mobile-network-experience), [nPerf coverage maps](https://www.nperf.com/en/map/DZ/2507480.Algiers/1226.Djezzy/signal)).
- **GPS/indoors accuracy & battery:** consumer-review methodology for kid trackers (indoor drift, fix time, real battery drain per day, app location-refresh lag) is published by [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/child-gps-trackers-wearables/gps-tracker-for-kids-a3099604963/) and [SafeWise (methodology stated)](https://www.safewise.com/kids-safety/gps-trackers/), [Mother & Baby tested round-up](https://www.motherandbaby.com/reviews/toddler-toys/gps-tracker-for-kids/).
- **Soak + telemetry:** continuous synthetic alarms (24/7 test device dialling in) with server-side delivery-receipt logging per channel; track p50/p95/p99 time-to-ack and **undelivered fraction per device model, OEM, Android version, carrier** — this is your only honest reliability number.
- **False-alarm measurement:** count alarms per 100 device-days by trigger type (strap, motion, GPS-loss) and define a "cry-wolf budget" (PERS practice of unlimited-false-alarm plans acknowledges false alarms as a first-class metric — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/caregiving/home-care/medic-alert-systems-options/)).
- **Destructive/anti-tamper tests:** strap cut, device shutdown, Faraday bag and (legally, in private tests only) jammer trials — measure worst-case "time until the system *notices* the device stopped talking."

## Reliability-architecture recommendation — 2 parents (Capacitor/Android) + 1 cheap cellular tracker + self-hosted Traccar

**Ordered by expected life-safety impact. [cheap] = days of work / few $ per month; [expensive] = significant dev time or recurring cost.**

1. **[cheap] Parallel, never serial, fan-out.** On any tracker alarm (SOS, JT808 alarm bit, or auto-trigger): simultaneously (a) push high-priority FCM to all guardian phones, (b) SMS all guardian numbers via a local Algerian A2P/SMS-gateway or (realistically) direct SMTP→SMS/mail2sms is unreliable — budget a small Twilio-class/local gateway fee, (c) place an automated voice call. No channel waits on another's ack.
2. **[cheap] "No ack = escalate" ladder with hard timers.** 60 s ack window per contact; parent 1 → parent 2 → backup list → SMS with pre-written "call 1548/17/112 now" incident summary + last position link. Log every transition. (Pattern from lone-worker systems + EN 50136 supervision.)
3. **[cheap] Treat silence as an alarm.** Device heartbeat: tracker keepalive every 1–2 min; missing keepalive while the child is "out of safe zone" (or during an active alert) → worst-case escalation instead of retries. Same for each parent phone: a phone that hasn't acked its watchdog for N min gets voice-call treatment, not push.
4. **[cheap] SMS-first firmware config on the tracker.** SMS-mode as peer channel (not just fallback): SOS triggers native voice-call + SMS from the device itself; Traccar receives GPRS when available. Choose a **2G SMS-capable JT808 tracker** (JT518 class fits). Zero extra cost, biggest single robustness win in Algeria.
5. **[cheap] Battery-killer hardening of the parent app.** Safety-app battery exemption (Google explicitly permits it), foreground service w/ `location` type, `setAlarmClock` watchdogs, WebSocket/MQTT own channel + HMS Push for non-GMS Huawei, onboarding that walks each OEM's dontkillmyapp whitelist. Measure with the benchmark app on every shipping device model.
6. **[cheap→moderate] Tamper/away-from-body events on the tracker.** Use what the hardware gives you: SOS, light-sensor (many TKSTAR units have an ambient-light/tamper flag), vibration, and "movement without GPS update" (possible GPS-loss/jamming signature). Map each to an alert type with its own escalation. Skin-electrode off-wrist detection = **later phase [expensive]**; strap-switch on bracelet housing = **cheap** once you custom-house.
7. **[moderate] Last-mile location honesty.** Every alert carries fix age + accuracy + cell ID; UI says "position from 3 min ago, ±100 m". Parents must not chase a ghost (Noonlight test failure mode). Cache trajectory in the tracker and server for police handoff.
8. **[moderate] Operator redundancy on the child's SIM.** Second-SIM variant of the tracker on a different Algerian network in the same cohort of users isn't possible cheaply, but **multi-IMSI IoT SIMs** ([Onomondo](https://onomondo.com/blog/what-is-multi-imsi-sim-for-iot/)) with Moroccan/EU profiles roam onto the best local network where allowed — verify Algerian roaming/regulatory terms; likely **expensive/unavailable domestically**, medium-term option.
9. **[moderate] Incident sheet for authorities.** One-tap PDF/WhatsApp-ready dossier (photos, timeline, GPS trace, suspected vehicle/last cell ID, the *verified* numbers 1548/17/14/1055/112 with "106 is wrong" corrected) + checklist of what to demand (immediate plainte, CCTV preservation within days, cell-site requisition via prosecutor). This doesn't send signals but buys back the 20–40 min the human chain loses.
10. **[expensive] Staffed fallback / auto-dial to a real person.** A monitoring-centre contract is what closes Noonlight's 4-minute gap; at MVP stage, substitute: an on-call family-group call tree (any member acks on behalf) + SMS blast to neighborhood group. Full centre = later.
11. **[expensive] Satellite path.** Iridium/ACN trackers are legally risky to import/operate in Algeria — **do not design around it**; revisit only if local regulation changes.
12. **[cheap, mandatory] Continuous synthetic-alarm telemetry** — the device-matrix reliability dashboard from §7; ship the p95 time-to-first-ack numbers internally every release. Life-safety claims must come from your own measured receipts, never from vendor FCM stats.

**Latency targets to put in the spec:** tracker→server alarm ≤ 30 s p95 (SMS mode), server→first-guardian-ack ≤ 45 s p95, full escalation complete ≤ 5 min, degraded-state declared ≤ 5 min of total silence. Given 76 % of murdered abducted children die within 3 h, holding the *human* chain to <5 min preserves the entire operational window.
